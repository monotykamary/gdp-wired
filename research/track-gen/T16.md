## First half (`c9d7f823`)

- `core/finite-model.ts` completes explicit solver cells to a finite default-zero
  state, recomputes derived counts, checks parameters and the whole current
  family, and runs the actual enabled request. Unsupported or refused evaluation
  is not a counterexample. Callers remain protected by the existing scheduler.
- `core/counts.ts` skips the other pass only for a certified finite CTI. A
  spurious pass-1 model is unknown, not a certified refutation. The ordinary
  failed-proof elimination policy is otherwise unchanged.
- Decisions, sparse refinement, part growth and final growth inherit one
  attempt end. No doubled decision allowance or restarted growth window remains.
  Caller priority still comes only from `supportBudget`.

Six strict paired probes, three against main `b788e195` and three against the
T-14 parent `8b44e4b3`, ended cleanly in 59.12–59.42 s. All 214 completed candidate
attempts were at most 1.010216 times their share. All verdicts were unknown.
The probe snapshot predates the final one-line sparse-refinement cap; the exact
committed source is separately covered by `t16-confirm`.

The first-half prediction was false: none of the completed models established a
relative CTI. The family remained 35, round zero did not finish, and safety was
not reached. Keep this failure; it is why the finite-state fallback is needed.

## Audit repair (`74dd9cc5`, `52b2382f`)

Lane D found that a single lazy count evaluation could run beyond cancellation,
and that a candidate finite CTI could violate declared facts. The count loop
now checks the active wall and optional attempt checkpoint every 256 iterations;
finite evaluation and the source interpreter propagate that checkpoint. Partial
sums are not cached. A dependency-free hook avoids importing the proof engine
into machine initialization. Every declared fact must hold before certification;
unsupported facts or derived sum-state return uncertified, never a CTI.

`runs/t16-audit-fixed/audit-fixed.log`: 20 tests / 82 assertions pass. Six exact
5,000,000-bound / 20 ms probes stop at 20.05–20.18 ms (three wall, three stop-only);
the false-fact `p=1` model is rejected. TypeScript passes; the combined lint stage
currently fails on two in-progress isolated-worker files, not the repair code.
The original repair's import-cycle failures remain in `t16-audit-fix-v1/` and
`t16-audit-quick/`. Lane D re-audit and final gates remain pending.

## Boundary sampling

`core/finite-frontiers.ts` proposes guard-enabled states, either side of a
width-two cube, and fields at their bounds using the law AST only. Count targets
are realized through underlying map bodies. Proposal mistakes can only lose a
sample: `finiteHoudini` still requires the entire predecessor family and an
actual post-state violation. The complete pass uses the normal wall-headroom
rule inside two seconds, returns already-certified partial drops on local
expiration, and never suppresses an earlier outer deadline.

`runs/t16-filter-summary.json` records three paired same-state comparisons on
an isolated Ryzen 9 9950X. All six walls are 1.9013 s or less and no solver query
is made. Random-only removes zero laws in the cap; boundary-first removes six,
including `CV at most 2`, every time. This catches only one of the seven costly
survivors, so it does not meet the five-law target. The generic tiny-state
fallback is in progress. No protocol name or hand invariant is used by the
production boundary generator.
