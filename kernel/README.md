# gdp-wired kernel: the prover out of the trusted base

| File | What |
| --- | --- |
| `kernel.ts` | Trusted (with `core/check.ts`). A goal is hypotheses that can't hold together. A proof is a tree of splits on linear atoms with a derivation at each leaf. The kernel reads the leaf's facts off the hypotheses under the path (ifs, mins and ∸ the path decides, conjunctions, the last disjunct still open, equal cells at keys the path proves equal, products of two rows) and asks `check.ts` whether the derivation refutes them. Two kinds of assumption: `pre` (params only: a precondition, at the start and every step) and `assume` (each request: at a step, never at the start). Every rule is a few moves of one algebra of runs (a run keeps the hypotheses met along it, each read where it was met, and the frame it ends at, every name a term over the first frame): `holds` (conditions where the run is), `reading` (a step's inputs named), `step` (a step taken from there, maps read-over-write), and a goal refuting a conclusion read at the end (`atEnd`) or across the run (`across`: plain names at the first frame, primed at the last). The induction rules: step machines (start + one step, every law in the set proven, ∀-laws instantiated at the keys the proof names), loops (invariants + a natural rank, or a tuple of ranks compared in order, that drops each turn; a rank may not read the turn's input), progress ("some request makes this happen", by naming the request and proving it allowed), step laws (`stepLawObligations`: for every allowed request, `then` relates the state before, by plain names, to the state and maps after, by primed ones, `s'` and `M'[k]`; the laws and the assumption are hypotheses, flags inlined; a history machine goes through `historyMachine`; with `steps: k`, k steps in a row, the laws and assumption before each, one `when` per step, the j-th request read as input@j), k-induction (`checkKInduction`: a plain law at the start, after each run of j < k steps from the start, and after any k steps in a row each starting where it holds; every goal but the start's a k-step law's, its `when`s and `then` built from the law alone; Lean twin `KInd.lean`'s `kind_sound`), and folds over binary trees (leaf + node from both children). A leaf may use products of two rows, including earlier product rows (any degree) |
| `qprove.ts` | Untrusted: `growQ`, the tree search for goals over ℚ. Each node's rows are the kernel's (`qLeafRows`); the incremental simplex over ℚ (`simplex.ts`, `integral` false) picks a conflict's rows and the elimination (`solver.ts`, no cut) refutes them exactly; otherwise a split on an open atom (a choice a term leaves open, a disjunction's or ≠'s comparison), and with none open the elimination decides the node, refuted or an exact model |
| `kernel.ts`: scope | Every name a machine, loop or fold reads is one it declares (params, inputs, state, maps, flags declared before, a law's own key); refused before any goal is made, since a goal would read a typo as one more free natural while the runtimes have no value for it |
| `kernel.ts`: histories | `historyMachine`: a machine that keeps a log of requests (`keep`, the recorded inputs read as `r.<input>`, a name prefix no other name may use). Sums are state, the sum over the log of a term of the record and params; queries are inputs, the sum over the log before the request of a term of the record, params and plain inputs; a relation `when ⇒ a ≤ b`, homogeneous over sums and queries with `when` over params and plain inputs, is proven for one record and then assumed at every step (both sides are sums over one list). `prune` adds a goal per query: a record whose order plus `after` is at most the request's adds 0, so the runtimes may drop it (they refuse requests out of order). Lean twin: `../lean-kernel/History.lean` |
| `kernel.ts`: sets | A request may carry whole maps (`inputMaps`); a step flag may hold at every key (`{op: 'all', key, c}`, its body reading no flag and the key only in key terms: alone, or plus or less a term that doesn't read it, as a map's key or a side of a comparison, so the runtimes decide it on finitely many breakpoints); a map may be written at every key (`{each, value}`). A goal reads a quantified flag f as its 0/1 variable `?f` and closes it: `?f ≤ 1`, `?f = 0 ∨ body(t)` at the keys t the proof names (instances under `?f`), and `?f = 1 ∨ ¬body(w?f)` at a fresh witness, which every real step satisfies. A later step of a k-step law reads `?f@j`, `w?f@j` and `Map@j`. A machine with a history takes none of these. (A quantifier written anywhere else in a step is named as a flag by `../core/dsl.ts`, and one inside a law pulled out as its key; one under a not in a law is some key, proven by `prove.ts` through a witness term, the law proven being the stronger one with the witness in place) |
| `kernel.ts`: nontermination | `checkRecur`: a loop has a run that never ends (NonTerm.lean's `NeverEnds`). The certificate is a closed recurrent set with its successor given: a condition over the state and params, a term per input over the state and params (the turn taken from the set), the params as values and a stem of turns' inputs from the start into the set. The start and the stem are evaluated exactly (bigint naturals, as Lean's `Nat`, map cells 0); the closure goal (`recurGoal`, built as Lean builds it: the set, and the witnessed turn failing `while` or the assumption or leaving the set) is a kernel goal. Lean twin: `../lean-kernel/NonTerm.lean` (`never_ends_of`) |
| `kernel.ts`: rationals | Goals over ℚ (Rationals.lean's twin, namespace `Q`): `QE` (variables, rational constants, +, − not truncated, × by a rational constant, min, max, if) and `QC` (≤, <, =, and, or, not, binary as Lean's). A term's linear form is a row with integer coefficients over a positive denominator, computed in bigint (`qlinear`; a constant's reduced num/den, as Lean's `Rat`); a split's yes side is lin ≤ 0, its no side the strict row −lin < 0 (no flip: over ℚ, lin ≤ 0 failing is lin > 0); `qfacts` gives strict rows for <, ¬≤ and ≠ under the path. No naturality row, no term column (an open choice gives no row; the search splits on it). `checkQTree`: each leaf's derivation over its rows checked by check.ts with `integral` false (no cut); a split the path decides already is refused. `qObligations`/`checkQMachine`: the induction rule over rational step machines, the precondition reading no input or state, no law an input. A row past 2^53 is left out of a leaf (weaker, never wrong). Lean twin: `../lean-kernel/Rationals.lean` |
| `kernel.ts`: liveness | `livenessParts`: "from leads to to" under fairness as step laws, while `from` holds and `to` doesn't: every request allowed keeps `from` or reaches `to`, and reaches `to`, lowers the rank (naturals compared in order) or keeps it with every region kept; some helpful request's region holds; and each helpful request, its named inputs fixed by its `when` and the rest free, reaches `to` or lowers the rank in its region. The conditions are built two parts per and/or, as Live.lean builds them. Lean twin: `../lean-kernel/Live.lean` (`leads_sound`, `sleads_sound`, over runs) |
| `kernel.ts`: several keys | A law (or step law) may hold at every two keys or more (`keys` after `forall`): its instances are tuples (kept flattened); its goal keeps the further keys as they are, fresh names nothing else reads, so the goal is about every value of them. Lean twin: `../lean-kernel/Sets.lean` |
| `kernel.ts`: functions | `fns`: maps no step writes, defined outside the rule (each with its JavaScript for the runtimes), read at any key; `facts`: laws over the params and the functions alone, assumed in every goal (start, step, step laws, progress) at the keys the proof names and never proven. A quantified flag or a write at every key reads no function at its bound key (a function's values don't stop where a map's cells do). Lean twin: `../lean-kernel/Sets.lean`, where a run starts where the facts hold |
| `prove.ts` | Untrusted. Where positions shift or a law is about several keys, assumes the every-key laws at each key the goal reads a map at, a law about several keys at the tuples that relate the goal's own keys to each other and to the rest (at most one place a key that reads none of them), and gives each step law its own instances. Picks the witness of each some-key (the first of a few candidate terms under which the laws prove) and the keys each fact is assumed at (every key a goal reads a function at, and the values that match a fact's reads to those keys). Grows trees: splits on whatever the hypotheses leave open, bisects bounded variables and branches on fractional coordinates when elimination is inexact, and multiplies rows when products appear (pairs of rows about the factors; degree three from rows times products of two variables; splits of a factor at zero or two factors against each other, so (x − y)² ≥ 0 becomes a product). A witness that multiplies out is a real model and stops the search. 20 s per goal, then `unknown`. `concretize` looks for a real counterexample with true arithmetic when products leave the witness abstract |
| `cdcl.ts` | Untrusted. CDCL over a linear one-step goal's atoms (prove.ts's growRoot tries it after a quick search of 16 nodes, with 3/4 of what is left, before the plain search and the cuts; not for k-step laws or products). The theory at each node is the kernel's rows at the trail, kept from the node before (`reader.ts`'s `sync`), the forms that came and went given to `simplex.ts`, an infeasible node's rows refuted exactly; a refuted node's clause is the decisions its rows need (`reader.ts`'s reasons), learned (first unique implication point, VSIDS, phase saving, Luby restarts; bound propagation from single-variable rows). The empty clause's resolution DAG is unfolded into a kernel tree: a resolution is a split, a leaf's derivation found again among the rows at its tree path or those rows refuted afresh |
| `reader.ts` | Untrusted. The kernel's reading of a goal (linear forms, Kleene truth, facts, term columns, leaf rows in kernel.ts's order, open atoms) along a trail, each value with its reason: a conjunction false needs one false part, a disjunction's last open part needs the others false, a term's value the decisions its choices took. A value with no open read is kept for as long as the trail keeps its reason; a change finds again only the values that read it (a hypothesis' facts by conjunct). `sync`: the leaf rows kept from one node to the next, made again only where the trail changed what they read, one row a form with the shortest reason among its copies |
| `simplex.ts` | Untrusted. A floating-point simplex in general form (a slack per linear form, bounds from a node's rows rounded to integers, Bland's rule), its tableau kept from node to node and begun again once it fills past 8 entries a row, the rows asserted and retracted by id as they come and go (`assert`, `retract`, `settle`); an infeasible row's bounds are a refutation's support, refuted exactly by core/solver.ts |
| `cdcl.test.ts` | cdcl.ts by brute force: every tree it returns checks and refutes only goals with no model, and it agrees with the plain search on what is satisfiable; `GDP_CDCL_VERIFY=1` compares reader.ts's rows with kernel.ts's at every node and every unfolded leaf |
| `run-progress.ts` | No lockout and progress: in-flight requests can always complete or be admitted, and an account under every cap gets its grant. False variants get stuck |
| `run-kernel.ts` | Every earlier machine proof as kernel-checked trees, plus tampered trees the kernel must reject |
| `run-more.ts` | Termination (ranks from templates), structural induction on trees, products (monomials as atoms), and integer completeness against brute force |
| `kernel.test.ts` | Soundness fuzz, sharded by `FUZZ_SEED` (`../modal/suite.json` runs 32 seeds on Modal). Random goals with ifs, mins, ∸, products and map reads: every accepted refutation is confirmed by brute force over concrete models, emptied derivations are rejected, and no linear goal ends `unknown`. Degree three: monotone polynomials, true and false, and leaves with random products of products; nothing with a model is accepted |
| `steplaw.test.ts` | Step laws: monotone counters, a `when`, maps at every key, a history's sums, refusals; k-step laws (two fitting adds, a reset then at most one amount, refusals); and soundness fuzz: random machines and step laws (and 2- and 3-step laws), every one the kernel accepts holds on every interpreter run (every run of k steps from every reached state), and false ones are caught |
| `sets.test.ts` | Sets (`../examples/sets.ts`, a derived authority): its laws, step laws and two-step laws proven and both broken variants refused where they break; the interpreter, the compiled code and the watcher agree on random runs; the refusals (a quantifier outside a step flag, a bound key in arithmetic, a carried map written, a history with any kind of set); a proof stops checking without its flag instances or without one step law's trees; and a fuzz of random machines over sets, every law the kernel accepts holding on every run |
| `history.test.ts` | Random machines with a history (and ceiling-shaped ones that need their relation): every law the kernel accepts holds on every run of the interpreter, which keeps the log, and so do the relations; histories outside the rule are refused |
| `probe-field-param.ts` | The counterexample Lean's proof exposed (a param named like a record field, r.t): now refused |
| `probe-products.ts` | Twelve true nonlinear laws (x² + y² ≥ 2xy, x ≤ y ⇒ x³ ≤ y³, …) proven, two false ones refuted with true arithmetic |

```sh
bun run-kernel.ts && bun run-more.ts && bun run-progress.ts && bun test kernel.test.ts history.test.ts steplaw.test.ts sets.test.ts cdcl.test.ts
```
