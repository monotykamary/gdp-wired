# gdp-wired lean-kernel: kernel.ts checked by Lean

The optional Lean path for the kernel, as `Cert.lean` is for `core/check.ts`: a Lean 4.34.0 twin of `kernel/kernel.ts`
(its tree check, all four of its induction rules, step flags, progress laws, step laws and k-step laws), proven sound, and an exporter that
sends the kernel's real trees to it. `Sets.lean` adds kernel.ts's sets, and `History.lean`, on top of it, the history rule.

| File | What |
| --- | --- |
| `Cert.lean` | The derivation checker and `Cert.sound`, as in `../research/lean/Cert.lean` but on sparse rows (each row its nonzero coefficients, in column order) |
| `Kernel.lean` | Trusted. Terms (var, const, +, ∸, × by a constant, ×, min, max, if, map reads) and conditions (≤, <, =, and, or, not) over naturals. A table names each column: a variable, a map's cell at a key over earlier columns, or a monomial (the product of earlier columns, factors sorted: variables, cells, open choices). `checkGoal`: path, Kleene truth, linear forms, facts, leaf rows, claimed cell equalities (re-checked), leaf squares (`leafSq`: each form t gives −t·t ≤ 0, whatever t's sign) and leaf products of two rows (p ≤ 0, q ≤ 0 give −p·q ≤ 0, a pair may name a square or an earlier product). Claimed rows are matched by coefficients, each first where its hint says (`leafAt`: a path atom, a hypothesis, a column, a cell pair, a term column; else among all of the leaf's rows), then the derivation is replayed by `Cert.check`. Rows are sparse throughout (`sp`: the nonzero coefficients, in column order), and certificates name the hypotheses they refute The induction rules build their goals in Lean, so kernel.ts's goal builders aren't trusted for them |
| `Cyclic.lean` | Trusted, on top of `Kernel.lean`. **One rule for every induction**: a cyclic proof is facts (conditions that hold at every value of their keys) and nodes, each a sequent (hypotheses ⇒ conclusion, for any maps, wherever the facts hold) with a rank (naturals compared in order), the facts it reads at terms, and its uses: another node (or itself) read at a substitution of its variables where a guard holds. Lean builds every goal from the nodes: a node's own (its hypotheses, the facts at its terms, what its uses give, against its conclusion), one per hypothesis of each node used (at the substitution), and one that the rank drops (`lexLtC`). `checkCyclic` checks each by `checkGoal`; a substitution may assign a variable a fact reads only where it is one of the fact's keys (`factsAvoid`) |
| `Progress.lean` | Trusted, on top of `Kernel.lean` (whose theorems it reuses unchanged). **Flags**: step terms read flag f as `.eq (.var f) (.const 1)`; `decideFlags` gives f's variable 1 when its condition holds before the step, else 0. Lean inlines flags itself (`inlineC`: the read becomes the condition, as kernel.ts's goal builder does; `inlineE`: any other read becomes `ite cond 1 0`) and proves inlining keeps the meaning. A machine with flags reaches, outside its flag variables, exactly the states its inlined machine reaches. **Progress**: Lean builds kernel.ts's two goals from the named request (the request is allowed; the step it causes makes `then` hold, primed names read the state and maps after the step) |
| `StepLaw.lean` | Trusted, on top of `Progress.lean`. **Step laws**: Lean builds kernel.ts's goal from the machine (Progress's goal with no request named: the inputs are any ι, the assumption with the flags decided is a hypothesis), so primed names read the state and maps after the step. The exporter inlines flags into `when` and `then`, which must then read no flag variable |
| `Steps.lean` | Trusted, on top of `StepLaw.lean`. **k-step laws**: Lean builds kernel.ts's goal as a symbolic run (a frame: each state variable a term over the first state, each map read a term over the first maps; a step composes the step terms, flags inlined, after the frame, maps read-over-write; before each step the laws, the assumption and that step's `when` at the frame; the j-th request's inputs read by fresh variables, `ren`). One invariant carries the proof: the frame denotes the real state after j steps outside the inputs, the fresh variables and the flags, and the real maps (`Denotes`, kept by `step_denotes`). The exporter sends one `when` per step, flags inlined |
| `History.lean` | Trusted, on top of `Sets.lean` (reusing `smachine_sound` unchanged). **Histories** (kernel.ts's `historyMachine`): a machine with sets (`SMachine`: maps, flags, sets a request carries, conditions and writes at every key) plus a log of the requests it keeps (`keep`, read with the flags decided). Sums are state variables defined as the sum over the log of a term of the record (`rvars`, the recorded inputs as r.f) and the params; queries are inputs computed at each request as the sum over the log of a term of the record, the params and the plain inputs; relations (when, a, b) with `when` over params and plain inputs and a, b homogeneous over sums and queries. `HReach` is that semantics, sums recomputed from the log and queries from the log before the request. Lean builds the plain machine (`HMachine.plain`: each sum from 0, plus its term at the request when kept; relations conjoined to the assumption) and each relation's record goal (`recordGoal`: a ≤ b for one record, its fields free), and checks kernel.ts's side conditions (`histOk`; sums, queries and relations read no map, flag, quantified flag or witness). A step reads its request through the machine's view (`sView`), its queries filled in from the log |
| `Sets.lean` | Trusted, on top of `Steps.lean`. The one twin every feature is built on (a machine with maps and flags is one with no sets). Its checks take a certificate (`Cert`, `checkIn`, `sound_in`) that may name the hypotheses its tree refutes, in its own order, each one Lean's goal has: an exporter lays its goal out its own way, and Lean checks only that it used hypotheses Lean builds. **Sets** (`SMachine`): maps a request carries (`carried`, any maps at each request), writes at every key (`every`: the value at each key, read with the key bound) and flags that hold at every key (`qflags`, decided classically in the meaning only: 1 when the body holds at every key). Lean builds kernel.ts's closed goals: each quantified flag ?f with `?f ≤ 1`, `?f = 0 ∨ body(t)` at the proof's keys (`qinsts`) and `?f = 1 ∨ ¬body(w)` at its witness (`wits`), the goal reading each step's request as its inputs, its flags' values and a failing key for each witness (`sReq`). A frame denotes the real state outside the extended request and a zone of variables, and the real maps outside the carried ones and the names later requests' maps are read by (`SDenotes`, `sstep_denotes`). The step goal of a law about every key runs from the state with the skolem set, which the machine reads nowhere (`skFresh`, `sNext_upd`). **Functions** are maps no step writes; `facts` are laws over the params and those maps alone: `SReach` starts where they hold, so they hold at every state (`facts_sreach`), and every goal assumes them at the proof's keys (`finsts`), read where the run begins (`facts_frame`); a fact about several keys (`pfacts`: a function of several arguments, a law between two points) is given at every value of its further keys (`SMachine.PFacts`, a premise of `SReach.start`), so it holds at every state (`pfacts_sreach`, `pfacts_frame`), and every goal assumes it at the proof's tuples as a fact about its first key (`pfinsts`, `pfactLaw`, `holdsOver_subst`). Any machine with functions goes through this file. **Several keys**: an entry may name further keys (`keys2`), variables nothing but laws about them read; it holds at every value of each (`holdsOver`, `SEntry.Holds`: equivalently, at every state that agrees but at those keys), and a frame assumes it at tuples as a law about the first key with the others read as the tuple's terms (`pairLaws`). The start and the step are lemmas (`sstart_case`, `sstep_case`) applied at every value of each further key in turn, which commutes with a step as the skolem does (`fresh_parts`) |
| `Live.lean` | Trusted, on top of `Sets.lean` and `StepLaw.lean`. **Liveness under fairness**: `leads_of_lex` (along any run: if P ∧ ¬Q is kept or Q reached with a rank of naturals never rising in order, and kept only with every region kept; some region holds; and each region's helpful step, taken infinitely often, lowers the rank there; then Q follows every P: a rank that never rises stops changing, `lex_stable`); `primeC`/`primeE` rename a condition's state variables and maps to their primed names, `primeC_eval` reads the renamed one as the condition after the step. Lean builds every goal as a step law from the liveness (`liveStay`, `liveCover`, `liveHelp`, each helpful request's named inputs fixed by its `when`), checks what the liveness reads (`liveOk`, `sliveOk`), and runs (`FRun`, `SRun`) are sequences of allowed steps from a reachable state; `leads_view` is the argument once, for both twins. Under weak fairness (`leads_wf_sound`, `sleads_wf_sound`): each helpful request also proven allowed in its region (`fAllowGoal`, `sAllowGoal`: the precondition, the laws, `when` and the closed quantified flags refute the assumption's failing; free inputs free or fixed by witness terms, the carried sets empty), so in the suffix where the rank stops it stays enabled, and a request taken whenever it stays enabled comes |
| `KInd.lean` | Trusted, on top of `Live.lean`. **k-induction** (`KInd`, `checkKInd`): a plain law L holds in every reachable state when it holds at the start (`kStartGoal`, the law's start goal), after every run of j steps from the start, 1 ≤ j < k, and after any k steps in a row from a reachable state, each starting where L holds. Each goal but the start's is `Sets.lean`'s k-step goal (`checkSKStep`), its `when`s and `then` built by Lean from L (`KInd.with`): a base reads its first state as the start (`startC`: each state variable its start term, which the start meets, `start_meets`) with L there and L before each later step; the step reads L before each of its k steps; `then` is L primed (`primeC`). The certificate gives only k, the trees, the instances and the names later requests are read by. `kind_sound`: by strong induction on the length of the run that reaches a state (`sreach_from`, `SPath`), a run shorter than k is a base, a longer one the step from the state k requests back, L holding at each state after it by the induction (`sallowed_rep`); the end read through the primed names is the state (`kind_then`, `law_reads`) |
| `NonTerm.lean` | Trusted, on top of `Live.lean`. **A run that never ends** (`NeverEnds l`: for some maps and some params meeting the precondition, a run of turns from the start that goes on forever; the negation of `loop_halts_of`'s conclusion, `halts_not_never_ends`). The certificate (`Recur`) is a closed recurrent set with its successor given: `set` (a condition over the state and params), `wit` (a term per input over the state and params: the turn taken from a state of the set), `start` (the params as values) and `stem` (each turn's inputs as values) from the start into the set, each state on the way held as its values on `dom`. `checkRecur` evaluates the stem (each turn meeting the guard and the assumption, its end in the set) and builds the closure goal (`recurGoal`: a state of the set whose witnessed turn fails the guard or the assumption, or ends outside the set), refuted by the kernel's tree (`checkIn`). The run after the stem is the witnessed turn again and again (`witRun`, by recursion): no choice is made |
| `Rationals.lean` | Trusted, on top of `Kernel.lean` (its row operations `ladd`, `lscale`, `lsub`, `same`, `lookup`) and `Cert.lean` (its `Row`, `Step`, `combineRow`, `addL`). **Linear arithmetic over ℚ** (namespace `Q`, core's `Rat`): Cert's rows read at a rational point (`evalQ`), each with a strictness (`Holds`: r < 0 or r ≤ 0); `replayQ`/`checkQ` replay Cert's steps with no cut (a cut needs an integer point: Cert's `cut_sound`), a combination strict when either row is, accepting a last row of zero coefficients with a constant > 0, or ≥ 0 and strict. Terms `QE`/`QC` over rationals (`evalE`, `evalC`); a term's linear form is a row over a positive denominator (`LF`, `linear`), a comparison's row `dif a b` a positive multiple of a − b; a path's no side is −l < 0 (`atomRow`); `facts` gives strict rows; a leaf claims rows found among its own (`claimOk`, strict only where the leaf's is). `checkGoal`, `sound`. Step machines over ℚ (`Machine`, `Reach`, `startGoal`, `stepGoal`, `checkMachine`, `machine_sound`), as Kernel.lean's scalar machines |
| `Ghost.lean` | Trusted, on top of `Live.lean`. **What a ghost means** (compose.ts's `ghost`): an `SGhosted` machine is the original's parts plus each ghost's start and step, read by no part of the original (`sghostOk`). `sghost_reach`: every reachable state of the original is, outside the ghosts, a reachable state of the ghosted machine, so its ghost-free laws are laws of the original (`sghost_laws`); `sghost_run`, `sghost_run_start`: every run of the original (from the start) lifts to a run of the ghosted machine taking the same requests; `sever_meaning`: an `ever` ghost is 1 exactly when some earlier step met its condition. **What "there exists" means** (core/exists.ts): `some_of_subst`, a law proven with witness terms σ in place (`substC σ c`) gives, at every state it holds in, values of its bound variables (the terms' values, written over the state by `updAll`) that satisfy c; `some_of_ghost`, a law of the ghosted machine over its ghosts gives the same at every reachable state of the original, the ghosts' values there the witness (through `sghost_reach`). The export states each such law as `∃ w1 .. wn : Nat, ...` (`<id>.someN`, and over named variables `<id>.lawN`) |
| `Refine.lean` | Trusted, on top of `Ghost.lean`. **What a refinement means** (compose.ts's `refinement`): Lean builds the composite from the implementation and the spec, both in the composite's names (`SRefined.full`: the spec's inputs read as the witness, each spec part kept where `stutter` holds, the sets a request carries shared by name) and checks neither reads the other's parts (`srOk`). `srefine_lift`: every reachable state of the implementation is, outside the spec's part, a reachable state of the composite; `srefine_project`: the spec's part of every reachable state of the composite is a reachable state of the spec (`sr_view`: the spec's view of a step agrees with the composite's with the witness put in); `srefines`: with the relation and the spec's precondition proven of the composite and its step law that the spec stutters or allows each request it is sent, every reachable state of the implementation is related by every law to a reachable state of the spec |
| `Bridge.lean` | Trusted, on top of `Sets.lean` and `Cyclic.lean`. **A cyclic proof of one of today's rules is a proof about the rule**: what `core/cyclic.ts`'s instances make of a rule is written here as a function of the rule's own syntax, a check compares an exported proof's facts and node against it, and a theorem carries the node to the rule's meaning. Machines (`machineNodes`): a run of an `SMachine` (no flag that holds at every key) as maps over time (each state variable and input x a map x@ read at the time, each map M and each map a request carries a map M@ whose cell u + k·Ω is M's cell k at time u, Ω = T + 1 so the pair decodes at every key); `cycCanon` the start, each step (flags inlined, with and without the assumption), each map's start and step (a write at one key read at the fact's key, `readsAtE`), the functions' facts; `cycMOk` the exported facts among those or true of every natural (`natC`), the node's hypotheses among the precondition, t ≤ T and T < Ω, its conclusion the law at t, and the names and maps apart. A reachable state ends a run (`reach_run`), whose encoding meets the facts (`canon_holds`). Recurrences (`recurNodes`): `recF` the values of f(0) = zero, f(n + 1) = next; `recOk` the exported equations are the recurrence's. Folds (`foldNodes`): a tree's table is its post-order (`BTree.flat`, each node after its children, `flat_node`); `foldOk` the exported table fact is the fold's (`foldTable`, with and without the assumption) |
| `BridgeRules.lean` | Trusted, on top of `Bridge.lean`, `Refine.lean` and `History.lean`. **The rest of today's rules carried by cyclic proofs**, each a theorem from a checked cyclic proof's node to what the usual rule's theorem states, with the claim's meaning in place of its certificate. Step laws and k-step laws (`cyc_kstep_sound`): a node over the run's last k steps up to time t (each time one step back, t − 1 − 1 …, as a step's fact reads the time before it), `then` with the plain names at t − k, each later request's inputs and the maps it carries at its time and the primed names at t (`kσ`, `kRd`), checked by `cycKOk`; the run a reachable state ends, extended by the k requests (`run_ext`). A helpful request allowed (`cyc_allow_sound`, `AllowHolds`: a node at the run's last request, which no step takes, its carried maps' cells 0). From those: liveness (`sleads_of_holds`, `sleads_wf_of_holds`), a history's laws through its plain machine (`hreach_plain`, the record goals still leaf goals), a ghosted machine's laws of the original (`cyc_ghost_holds`), a refinement (`srefines_of_holds`). Flags that hold at every key: the machine read as its sk machine (`SMachine.sk`, each such flag and its witnesses inputs a request names), its runs whose every request is decided as a goal reads it (`sk_step`, `sk_run`), the flags' closing conditions facts of the encoding (`cycQFacts`, true on those runs by Sets.lean's `closing_true`: `qfacts_hold`); `cyc_qmachine_sound`, `cyc_qkstep_sound`, `cyc_qallow_sound` |
| `Data.lean`, `data.ts` | On top of `Cyclic.lean`; nothing in it is trusted beyond `cyclic_sound`. **A datatype law's cyclic proof is a theorem about the datatype as declared** (`core/data.ts`): `pk a b = (a + b)² + b`, the pairing a function of several arguments is read at, injective (`pk_inj`) with a left inverse `unpk` (chosen classically); `cun`, the naturals onto the pairs in order of their sum, and `cpair` its inverse (`cun_cpair`, `cpair_cun`, each part at most the whole); `tup`/`tupk`, a tuple of naturals packed in one. `data.ts` (untrusted) writes, for each proof, the datatypes as Lean `inductive`s, the functions by pattern matching (Lean checks their recursion), a bijection between each datatype and the naturals (`enc`, `dec`, `dec_enc`, `enc_dec`: those of no field their tag, the rest z + m·q + r), the model (each map of the encoding as what the facts say it is: a function's map at a key, the function at the arguments the key pairs), a proof of every fact of the proof true of that model (`facts_hold`, by unfolding and cases on the values a fact reads), and the law as a theorem about the Lean definitions from `cyclic_sound` at the law's node (`datalen.length_append : len (append xs ys) = len xs + len ys`) |

The theorems (`#print axioms`: `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound` for each; no `sorry`, no `native_decide`):

| Theorem | Says |
| --- | --- |
| `Kern.sound` | an accepted goal's hypotheses hold for no naturals and no maps |
| `Kern.cyclic_sound` | cyclic proofs: if `checkCyclic facts nodes`, then for any maps, at every environment where the facts hold, every node's hypotheses imply its conclusion. Well-founded induction on the rank (`lexLt_wf`: tuples of naturals of one length, compared in order) |
| `Kern.machine_sound` | scalar step machines: if `checkMachine m ps`, every law holds in every `Reach` state. `Reach.start` needs the precondition `pre`; `preOk` checks `pre` reads no input and no variable the start or a step assigns |
| `Kern.mmachine_sound` | machines with maps: one write per map per step (read-over-write as `if`), maps starting at declared constants, laws about every key proven at a fresh skolem and assumed before the step at the keys the proof names. `pre` may not read maps |
| `Kern.inline_evalE`, `inline_evalC` | inlining flags keeps the meaning: a term or condition inlined, read in a state, equals the original read once the flags are decided (`propext` only) |
| `Kern.fmachine_sound` | scalar machines with flags (`FMachine`): if `checkFMachine f ps` (flag conditions read no flag, laws read no flag, and `checkMachine` of the inlined machine), every law holds in every `FReach` state. Via `freach_plain`: each `FReach` state agrees, outside the flag variables, with a `Reach` state of the inlined machine |
| `Kern.fmmachine_sound` | the same with maps (`FMMachine`, `FMReach`, `fmreach_plain`; writes evaluated with the flags decided) |
| `Kern.progress_sound` | progress over machines with maps and flags: if the laws' proof checks (`checkFMMachine`) and `checkProgress f ps pp` (the request names only inputs, no primed name is an input, `then` reads no flag, and both goals check), then `ProgressHolds f pp`: in every `FMReach` state where `when` holds, the named request (ι := its terms, read in that state) satisfies the assumption once the flags are decided, and `then` holds, its primed names read the state and maps after that step and its plain names the state before (with the request's inputs). `progress_reach`: the state after it is reachable too |
| `Kern.steplaw_sound` | step laws over machines with maps and flags: if the laws' proof checks (`checkFMMachine`) and `checkStepLaw f ps sl` (`when` and `then` read no flag, and the goal checks), then `StepLawHolds f sl`: in every `FMReach` state, every request (inputs ι) that satisfies the assumption once the flags are decided and that `when` admits takes a step where `then` holds, primed names reading the state and maps after it. A history machine's step laws go through its plain machine (`historyMachine`) |
| `Kern.kstep_sound` | k-step laws over machines with maps and flags: if the laws' proof checks and `checkKStep f ps sl` (one `when` per step, the first step reading its own inputs and each later one fresh variables, distinct, read by nothing but `then`; nothing a frame reads touches them or a flag; the goal checks), then `KStepHolds f sl`: from every `FMReach` state, every k requests in a row that are allowed (flags decided) and meet their `when` end in a state where `then` holds, plain names reading the first state and request, `input@j` the j-th request, primed names the last state and maps |
| `Kern.hmachine_sound` | machines with a history (`HMachine`, over a machine with sets): if `checkHMachine h ps rs` (`histOk`, a checked record goal per relation, and `checkSMachine` of the plain machine), every law holds in every `HReach` state, sums read as their sums over the log. Via `hreach_sreach`: every `HReach` state is an `SReach` state of the plain machine, the relations holding of each request because they hold of each record (`homog_sum`: a homogeneous term over sums of one list is the sum of the term; `sum_le_map`) |

With cyclic certificates the trusted base is the leaf check (Kernel.lean, Cert.lean), the cyclic rule (Cyclic.lean) and
the bridges (Bridge.lean, BridgeRules.lean): what each rule claims (Sets.lean's `SReach` and `SKStepHolds`, History's
`HReach`, Live's runs and fairness, Ghost's and Refine's machines) is still the twin's definitions, the statements the
theorems are about; the rules' own certificates and their checks (`checkSMachine`, `checkSKStep`, `checkFold`, ...) are
not read. What stays a rule: the step from a liveness's step laws to its claim along infinite runs (Live.lean's
`leads_view`, `lex_stable`, `loop_halts_of`). A cyclic proof's node is a sequent at one environment: its conclusion a
condition over finitely many values, proven by well-founded induction on a rank. "`from` is followed by `to`" is about
an infinite run, an existential over its times under fairness (itself a ∀∃ over the run), and no node states it; the
drop of the rank along the run is a step law (a node), but that a rank of naturals compared in order stops dropping and
the run then takes a helpful step needs an argument over the run itself. Covering it would take a cyclic rule over
infinite runs (nodes whose conclusions hold eventually, ranked by the run's own time), not this one.

In TypeScript the default (`GDP_CERT=usual` turns it off) makes the same certificates (kernel/prove.ts), each
translated from the usual proof: laws (a machine with flags that hold at every key through its sk machine), histories
through their plain machine, step laws and k-step laws (over a map too: a later step's map the usual goal's term over the
cells at t − k), requests allowed, recurrences and folds. Where a translation isn't made the usual trees are the
certificate, checked as before, and the proof is counted uncovered with why; `GDP_CERT_SEARCH=1` searches those first,
within `GDP_CERT_MS` (10,000 ms). The export searches within the same deadline. Left on the usual rule in
`runs/modal/cover/last-default` (the suite by default): 4 machines (a law reading a map at a key read from a map), 8
k-step laws (not translated), and the liveness (36) and loop (79) claims along infinite runs above.

**kernel.ts gap found here, since fixed:** `loopObligations` accepted ranks that read the turn's inputs. `forever`
(state x from 0, input w, while x < w, x := x + 1, rank w ∸ x) passed kernel.ts's check, yet the loop runs forever when
every turn sends w = x + 1. kernel.ts now refuses such ranks, and a `while` that reads the turn's input; `Trees.lean`
writes `forever`'s halting out as a liveness by hand, and `sliveOk` rejects it because its `to` and its rank read an input.

Not covered by the twin:
- **Flags inside `then`** of a progress law: kernel.ts allows them (read at the request), `checkProgress` refuses them.
  Flags inside a flag's condition are refused by both.
- **Progress over scalar `Machine`s** goes through `FMMachine` with no maps (in-flight is exported that way).
- **The `natural` check** has no counterpart because Lean's terms are natural by construction (constants and factors
  are `Nat`).
- **Histories**: Lean's log holds whole request environments; sums and queries read only the recorded inputs from them
  (through `fieldSub`), as kernel.ts's records hold only those. `histOk` also asks that the precondition read no input,
  flag or field and no map, which kernel.ts's `preOf` already implies for every machine.
- Lean is stricter than kernel.ts in places, never looser: a law may not read a flag, a progress request's keys must be
  inputs and primed names may not be, a product pair or square that fails to
  multiply rejects the leaf (kernel.ts adds an empty row, and refuses an open choice as a factor), and a monomial
  column must be present for every product of two columns a row needs.
